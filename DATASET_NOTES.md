## 3. Data types and null behaviour

Full per-column statistics are in `reports/data_dictionary.md`.

- **Null marker:** a null is always an **empty field**. No other marker (`NA`, `null`, `-1`) is used as a null [DATA]. Read every column as a string first, so that pandas does not turn codes into floats or IDs into NaN-able floats.
- **Numbers written as `N.0`:** most numeric codes are written that way (`card2`, `card3`, `card5`, `addr1`, `addr2`, `C*`, most `D*`, `V*`, `id_*`). Keep **`addr1` as the literal string** (`"444.0"`), because the case-pack trigger text quotes it that way ("billing region 444.0").
- **Integer-written columns:** `TransactionID`, `TransactionDT`, `card1`, `first_fraud_txn_id`, `flagged_txn_id`, `n_txns`. Answer files require IDs **as strings**, e.g. `"3514030"` [README: "list of strings"].
- **`risk_score`:** decimal with 2 places, 0.01 … 0.99, 99 distinct values, never blank in transactions [DATA]. In `case_pack.csv` it is blank for the 9 non-`risk_score` triggers (check `benchmark:risk_score_present_iff_risk_trigger`).
- **Timestamps:** `ts`, `opened_at` and `closed_at` all use `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`. All values parse (checks `time:*_parseable`).

Foreign keys, all verified with zero orphans:
- `closed_cases.txn_ids` (pipe-separated) → `TransactionID`. There are 14,955 references, each transaction appears in at most one case, and every listed transaction belongs to the case's `customer_id`.

### Card ID derivation [INFERRED]

The README says only that `customer_id` is "derived from the card issuer field; one customer can have several cards. Card IDs in the cases look like `C01234-K1`". `transactions.csv` has **no card column**. Rule tested:

> `card_id = customer_id + "-K" + n`, where `n` is the 1-based rank of the transaction's `card6` value among that customer's distinct `card6` values, sorted lexically with blank (`""`) first.

- **Evidence:** it reproduces **100%** of the 14,975 labelled (transaction, card) pairs: every closed-case transaction plus all 20 flagged transactions. Every closed-case `card_id`, all 92 connected card IDs and all 20 case-pack cards exist under it.
- **Alternatives tested:** keys `card4`, `card4+card6`, `card3+card4+card6` and `card2…card6`, each ordered by first-seen, by frequency and lexically. All other keys scored 34–39%, except `card4` in lexical order at 99.8%.
- **Result:** 14,317 cards. 12,793 customers have 1 card, 756 have 2, and 4 have 3.
- **Unverified:** ordering among `debit`/`debit or credit` (2 customers) and `charge card`/`credit` (1 customer). No label covers these.
- **Temporal caveat (§7):** the numbering is computed over all six months, so a K-number can encode a card that first appears later.

## 5. Timestamp and epoch semantics

- **`ts` [README]:** real timestamp, `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`, from 2016-07-02 to 2016-12-31.
- **`TransactionDT` [README]:** seconds from the dataset start, disguised with small offsets.
- **Epoch [DATA]:** `ts == 2016-07-02 00:00:00 + TransactionDT` seconds on **every** row (check `time:ts_equals_origin_plus_TransactionDT`). The disguise offsets were applied before `ts` was produced, so `TransactionDT` adds no information. Use `ts`.
- **Range [DATA]:** 2016-07-02 00:02:21 … 2016-12-31 23:58:54. The file is sorted by `ts` and `TransactionID` together.
- **Timezone:** not stated. Treat all timestamps across files as the same naive, bank-local clock [PROPOSED].
- **Ties [DATA]:** 31,156 rows share a timestamp with another row, and 314 of them are on the same card. Order events by `(ts, TransactionID)`.
- **Case timestamps:** `opened_at` and `closed_at` use the same format. Each case-pack `opened_at` falls a whole number of hours (1–6 h) after its flagged transaction [DATA].
- **`D1`–`D15` [README]:** Vesta time deltas in days, with values up to 879. They refer to history before the dataset window, and they are not re-based to `ts`.
- **Answer dates:** `sar.activity_dates` use `YYYY-MM-DD`, taken from `ts` [README].

## 6. Transaction history boundaries

| Month (2016) | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Transactions | 130,295 | 93,786 | 92,903 | 100,420 | 84,805 | 88,533 |

- **Periods [README]:** July–October is the history period (closed cases); November–December is the exam period (the case pack).
- **No left history [DATA]:** the dataset holds nothing before 2016-07-02. A customer's "normal behaviour" is limited to what falls inside the window.
- **Future data is present [DATA]:** transactions dated after each benchmark case exist in the file: 7,252 of them for HHG-004 and up to 139,949 for HHG-017 (§12). A loader must not expose them to the investigation.
- **Customer sizes are skewed [DATA]:** the median is 4 transactions per customer, but 104 customers have more than 1,000 and the largest has 14,932. `customer_id` comes from an issuer field, so a large "customer" may not be one person. Baselines must be computed per card, and must be robust to this.

## 7. Closed-case timing and temporal-leakage rules

**Facts [DATA]:**
- `opened_at` runs 2016-07-02 07:17:26 … 2016-11-02 02:00:37. `closed_at` runs 2016-07-04 02:10:20 … 2016-11-06 23:39:58.
- Cases stay open 16–142 h (median 69 h). There are no inverted dates.
- **The README says "July to October", but 25 cases were opened and 158 closed in November** (warning `time:closed_cases.within_july_october`).
- Every closed case was closed **before the earliest benchmark case** (HHG-017, opened 2016-11-12 00:46:24; gap ≥ 5 days). Check `time:closed_cases.all_closed_before_first_benchmark`.
- No closed case is "opened but not yet closed" at any benchmark `opened_at`.
- Every closed-case transaction is at or before that case's `opened_at`. The latest one is 2016-10-31 23:39:58.

**Leakage-prevention rules [PROPOSED].** Let `as_of` = the case's `case_pack.opened_at` (§12).

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| T1 | A transaction, and its identity record and `risk_score`, is visible only if `ts ≤ as_of`. Every graph query takes `as_of` as a mandatory parameter; there is no unfiltered variant. |
| T2 | A closed case is retrievable only if `closed_at ≤ as_of`. Its outcome, pattern and notes are unknowable before closure. The filter is enforced per query even though it is currently always true, because agent-written cases join the same memory. |
| T3 | An agent-written case, from another benchmark or the optional monitoring run, is retrievable only if its own `as_of` is earlier than the current `as_of`, and it may contain only evidence visible at its own `as_of`. |
| T4 | Aggregates are computed from visible rows only: per-card baselines, home region, known devices, "device New for this account", counts, sequences, and `NEXT` ordering. |
| T5 | Graph algorithms run on the `as_of`-filtered subgraph, never on the full graph: shared-device and shared-region clustering, community detection, centrality. Precomputed global results are forbidden. |
| T6 | A card or customer "exists" for the investigation only if it has a visible transaction. **`card_id` K-numbers are opaque labels.** They are derived from the full six months, so at 2016-11-12, 225 customers' K-numbers would differ if derived from visible data only, and 1,243 cards first appear later. Never reason from a K-number (e.g. "K2 exists, so they have two cards"). |
| T7 | Unnamed row-level features (`C*`, `D*`, `V*`, `M*`, `id_*`) are used only for visible transactions. Whether Vesta computed them point-in-time is not documented (§14 Q6). |
| T8 | The simulated responses in `evidence_requests` are recorded as assumptions. They may never be derived from future transactions or from closed cases on the same activity. |
| T9 | Tests: every investigation tool gets a test proving that rows after `as_of` are excluded, including the row one second after `as_of`. The validator's `asof_availability` counts are the reference. |

## 8. Structure of `case_pack.csv`

8 columns and 20 rows, one per benchmark case.

| Column | Type | Observed [DATA] | Meaning [README] |
|---|---|---|---|
| `case_id` | string | `HHG-001` … `HHG-020`, in order | Case ID; also the answer file name |
| `opened_at` | timestamp | 2016-11-12 00:46:24 … 2016-12-29 07:53:54 | When the alert was opened (used as `as_of`, §12) |
| `trigger_type` | enum | `risk_score` 11, `customer_report` 8, `analyst_request` 1 | Why the alert exists |
| `trigger_text` | string | 3 templates (below) | Alert text |
| `flagged_txn_id` | integer as string | 3450629 … 3583368 | "Where the alert fired. It is not necessarily where the fraud started, and it may not be fraud at all" |
| `card_id` | string | 20 distinct; K1 or K2 | Card on which the alert fired |
| `customer_id` | string | 20 distinct | Cardholder |
| `risk_score` | decimal or blank | Blank for the 9 non-`risk_score` triggers; 0.52 … 0.90 otherwise | "Filled only for risk-score triggers" |

Trigger text templates:
- **`risk_score`:** "Real-time model scored transaction `<id>` (`$<amt>`, online | in billing region `<addr1>`) at `<score>`. Review and decide." In-person flagged transactions quote `addr1`.
- **`customer_report`:** "Customer `<cust>` message: 'I never made this `$<amt>` purchase. Please check my card.' Refers to `<id>`." This is a customer **dispute**, which triggers the §3a "open a case" rule.
- **`analyst_request`** (HHG-014 only): "several cards this month show purchases from the same unusual device profile…"

The flagged transaction's `risk_score` in `transactions.csv` is visible for every case, including customer reports (0.25–0.48 on those).

### Constraints stated in the README
- Every ID must exist in the dataset; "made-up IDs score zero".
- A `legitimate` verdict means an empty `affected_txn_ids`, `exposure_usd` = 0 and `sar.file` = false.
- `uncertain` earns full credit on ambiguous cases if the actions follow R1 and R8.
- Action names and routes must use the policy's exact identifiers.
- Actions are ordered by what happens first.
- The case is also written to TigerGraph, so later investigations can retrieve it.

### Problems in the README's example (illustrative only; not a template for values)
- `exposure_usd` of 268.43 does not equal the sum of the narrated amounts (1.10 + 2.40 + 0.95 + 259.98 = 264.43).
- The evidence and narrative refer to card `C00377-K1`, but the rest of the example uses `C00877-K1` as the *connected* card.
- The transaction IDs (`T0412877`) and device ID (`D000731`) are not in dataset format. Real transaction IDs are 7-digit numeric strings, and device profiles have no dataset ID.
- The example is labelled HHG-017, but its dates (2016-11-14) and customer differ from the real HHG-017 (opened 2016-11-12, customer C04570).

Conclusion: copy the example's **shape**, never its values or ID formats.

## 10. Fraud policy rules and documented fraud patterns [README]

### Actions and approval routes (exact identifiers)

| Route | Actions |
|---|---|
| `auto` | `ALLOW_TRANSACTION`, `MONITOR_CARD`, `MONITOR_CONNECTED_CARDS`, `WARN_CUSTOMER`, `VERIFY_WITH_CUSTOMER`, `STEP_UP_AUTH`, `GENERATE_REPORT`, `CREATE_CASE`, `ESCALATE_TO_ANALYST`, `CLOSE_NO_FRAUD` |
| `L1` (team lead) | `DECLINE_TRANSACTION`; `BLOCK_CARD` when exposure ≤ $2,500 |
| `L2` (fraud manager) | `BLOCK_CARD` when exposure > $2,500; `BLOCK_ALL_CARDS` always; `FILE_REPORT` always |

Only `auto` actions may be executed by the agent. `L1` and `L2` actions are recommended and wait for a human.

### Rules

| Rule | Condition | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | A single signal (including risk score alone) and fraud probability < 0.70 | `VERIFY_WITH_CUSTOMER` or `STEP_UP_AUTH` before any block. Blocking on one signal is a breach |
| R2 | Customer denies | `BLOCK_CARD` + `CREATE_CASE`; add `FILE_REPORT` if exposure > $1,000 or the case connects to a shared device profile or another card's fraud |
| R3 | Customer confirms | `CLOSE_NO_FRAUD`; note the confirmation |
| R4 | No reply within 24 h | `MONITOR_CARD` + `DECLINE_TRANSACTION` for pending authorizations; escalate if exposure > $500 |
| R5 | Card testing: ≥ 3 small online authorizations on one card within 1 h, then a larger purchase | `DECLINE_TRANSACTION` + `STEP_UP_AUTH`; `BLOCK_CARD` if a purchase over $100 has already cleared |
| R6 | Shared origin: several cards with fraud from the same device profile, billing region or recipient email in one window | Name the shared element; `CREATE_CASE` + `FILE_REPORT` + `MONITOR_CONNECTED_CARDS` for every card that shares it |
| R7 | Disputed but matches the customer's own recurring pattern | `CREATE_CASE` + `VERIFY_WITH_CUSTOMER` + `WARN_CUSTOMER`; do not block |
| R8 | Verdict `uncertain` and exposure > $500, or conflicting evidence | `ESCALATE_TO_ANALYST` |
| R9 | Coordinated or repeated abuse across customers that fits no known pattern | `CREATE_CASE` + `FILE_REPORT` + `ESCALATE_TO_ANALYST`; describe the pattern in your own words |
| R10 | `BLOCK_ALL_CARDS` | Only if ≥ 2 of the customer's cards show confirmed fraud, or credentials are confirmed compromised |

Other policy sections:
- **§3a, when to open a case (`CREATE_CASE`):** fraud probability ≥ 0.30, **or** any evidence request, **or** any customer dispute.
- **§3a, when to file a report (`FILE_REPORT`):** fraud confirmed or strongly suspected **and** at least one of: exposure > $1,000; a connection to a shared device, shared region cluster or another customer's fraud; a coordinated or undocumented pattern (R9). A report always has a case behind it.
- **§3b:** record both the initial and the final recommendation, and what changed between them.
- **§4, exposure:** the sum of |amount| over the identified episode, including the flagged transaction.
- **§5, extra evidence:** customer validation, step-up authentication and analyst information need no approval. Responses are not provided, so simulate them and state the assumption.
- **§6, when to stop:** stop when probability is ≥ 0.85 or ≤ 0.15 with at least two independent pieces of evidence, **or** a verification response settles the question, **or** further steps would not change the decision. Stopping too early and too late are both penalised.
- **§7, explanations:** every recommendation must cite its evidence, why more evidence was requested, and the rule number.

### The five documented patterns

| # | `pattern` value | Definition [README] | Policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `card_testing` | ≥ 3 tiny online authorizations (often < $5), then a larger purchase; the sequence itself confirms it | R5 |
| 2 | `card_not_present_fraud` | Online use without the card; amounts and products don't fit history, often a burst of 2–4 within 48 h. One unusual purchase is ambiguous: verify | R1–R4 |
| 3 | `card_not_present_new_device` | Pattern 2, plus identity marks the device `New` for the account, sometimes behind a proxy. Stronger, but still not proof | – |
| 4 | `out_of_region_use` | Card-present purchases in a billing region with no history while normal activity continues at home. Several days in one new region is a trip, not a clone | R2, R3 |
| 5 | `account_takeover` | Mixed-channel activity inconsistent with the cardholder, often with device and match-flag anomalies | – |

The README warns that other patterns exist in the data and that finding them is scored (`undocumented`, R9).

### What the closed-case history shows [DATA]
- **Case counts:** `card_not_present_fraud` 1,404; `account_takeover` 1,205; `card_not_present_new_device` 1,076; `out_of_region_use` 955; `card_testing` 16; `undocumented` 9; `none` (cleared) 900.
- **Actions taken:** only 3 combinations occur. `CREATE_CASE|BLOCK_CARD` 4,268; `CREATE_CASE|BLOCK_CARD|FILE_REPORT` 397; `VERIFY_WITH_CUSTOMER|CLOSE_NO_FRAUD` 900. Approval routes are not recorded.
- **Reports and policy agree:** among confirmed fraud, `report_filed = Yes` in **exactly** the cases with exposure > $1,000 (393) or with connected cards (4). That matches §3a.
- **Cleared cases:** each has one transaction, exposure 0, and one of 3 note templates: "confirmed travel to the billing region" (716); "confirmed the purchase from a new phone" (158); "confirmed the purchase … consistent with their stated intent" (26).
- **Risk scores on closed-case alerts:** cleared 0.81–0.99 (mean 0.88); confirmed fraud 0.01–0.98 (mean 0.48). This bears out "a risk score is a reason to look, never a verdict".
- **Two `undocumented` sub-types appear in the notes:**
  - (a) A **shared-device ring**: a Samsung SM-G935F on Chrome for Android behind an anonymous proxy, new to each account. It spans 4 cases (CC-2649, CC-2971, CC-2985, CC-3035) and 24 cards listed in `connected_card_ids`.
  - (b) **Threshold avoidance**: four online purchases within 40 minutes, each just under $500 (5 cases, exposure about $1.9k each).
